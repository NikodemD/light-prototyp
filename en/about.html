LIGHT makes residents’ health and well-being part of spatial planning and the transition towards Positive Energy Districts.

Challenges

  • Compact urban development often conflicts with access to daylight and solar radiation.
  • Higher density intensifies the urban heat island, noise and poorer air quality.
  • Energy systems, blue-green infrastructure and mobility are rarely planned together.
  • Renovation has to remain affordable and socially just.
  • Planning tools seldom link energy performance with physical and mental health.

Proposed solution

  • Three living labs: Kartuzy, Lund and a virtual lab in Innsbruck.
  • Further development of CitySim and DALEC, with participatory scenario modelling.
  • A regenerative finance frame for social, environmental and financial benefits together.

Equality and DNSH

Women make up more than half of the research team and lead the project coordination. The consortium limits its carbon footprint through land travel, online meetings, green procurement and less printed material.
